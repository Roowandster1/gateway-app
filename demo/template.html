Why this is not a chatbot answer

  • Food is sold in packs, not grams. A recipe needing 70g of lentils costs you a whole 99p bag. Cost is computed over whole packs bought, which is what forces the plan to reuse an ingredient rather than open a second one.
  • Leftovers split two ways. Rice and oil carry over and make next week cheaper; chicken and bread rot, and the objective is penalised for creating them. Anything outliving the plan counts as an asset, not waste.
  • When nothing fits, it says so and prices the floor. infeasible with the binding constraint named is a real answer, not an error.
  • Prices are seed estimates with a source and a date, shown as unverified until someone corrects one in the aisle. Nothing is guessed.
  • Tap a meal for the method. Those steps are the only part written by a language model, and they carry no amounts — a checker rejects any step that states a quantity or names an ingredient the shop did not cover.
